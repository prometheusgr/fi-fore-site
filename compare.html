How fi-fore compares

Checked on 27 November 2026. Prices are in US dollars and change; the linked pages at the bottom are the authority.

A spreadsheet, a subscription, and a free open-source app all solve part of the same problem. This page lays out what each one does, using what the products say about themselves, and says where fi-fore is the weaker choice. We wrote it; read it with that in mind, and tell us if a cell is wrong.

Where others do better

  • Actual Budget is open source today, offers optional end-to-end encryption, and has been around far longer. If inspecting the code and a mature community matter most to you, it has both and fi-fore does not yet.
  • Monarch and YNAB have mobile apps (iOS and Android) and a hosted service that works with no setup. Monarch connects through three data providers; fi-fore reaches banks through SimpleFIN or CSV only. fi-fore is a desktop app with no mobile app.
  • Empower has a free dashboard that covers investment accounts and net worth. fi-fore's focus is the household's monthly plan, not an investment portfolio.
  • A spreadsheet does anything you can build, costs nothing extra, and you already know how it works.

Where fi-fore is different

  • The plan comes first: every transaction is checked against the plan each week, instead of the plan being a budget you look at afterward.
  • The 50/30/20 split shows on the forecast, not only on what already happened.
  • Scenarios such as a refinance or a mortgage are built in, on your own data, on your own machine.

How we checked
