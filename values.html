What we won’t trade

Eight values decide what fi-fore does when two good things conflict. Each has one line and the place you can check it. Where a proof is not there yet, the entry says so.

  1. Your data, your machine

    Local by default. Any copy elsewhere is the household’s choice, and labeled as such.

    Check it: who can read your money, and for how long

  2. Facts, not prescriptions

    The standard is opinionated; the reading is factual. We measure, the household decides.

    Check it: the comparison states where others do better, in plain words. In the app, the Budget rules screen shows each reading against its rule.

  3. Honest labels

    “Can’t read it” and “won’t read it” are never blurred.

    Check it: the two tiers, named as such; hosted is not yet available

  4. The free core is the product

    Never a funnel, never gated.

    Check it: the price row. The desktop app has no subscription and no paid feature.

  5. Leaving is trivial

    A plain SQLite file you can download and take anywhere, and the same app runs anywhere.

    Check it: download the app, then use Backup & Restore to save your database file.

  6. Open by default

    Trust comes from inspection, not promises: the source opens under AGPL-3.0.

    Check it: the open source row. The source is not public yet, so this one cannot be checked today.

  7. AI serves, never decides

    The household’s own endpoint, local first. It explains readings and drafts changes; the household approves every one.

    Check it: no AI service is among the places your data goes

  8. We ask, we don’t watch

    Decisions start from these values. Evidence comes only from what a household chooses to tell us (opt-in sharing, interviews, issues), never from counting people who didn’t agree: not in the app, not on the site.

    Check it: this site uses no trackers, cookies or analytics, and a test in the site repository fails if one is added. How to tell us what you want.
