Your plan, checked every week

A budget tells you what you spent; a forecast tells you what your balance will be. fi-fore asks one thing about each charge: was it planned, and is the plan still true?

  1. New transactions come in

    From your bank through SimpleFIN Bridge, at most once a day, or from a CSV file your bank exports. Rows already stored are flagged as duplicates before you import.

  2. Days says where you stand

    The check-in strip at the top of Days reads like Last reconciled 7 days ago · 4 new · 2 matched to your plan. “To look at” takes you straight to the charges that need you.

  3. Each charge is matched to the plan

    fi-fore suggests the plan item each transaction belongs to, scored by amount, category and description. Confirm them one at a time, or the confident ones together.

  4. A charge that wasn’t planned joins the plan

    Create Forecast puts it in the plan with the amount filled in and links the charge to it. The ratio moves with it.

Days holds this month’s transactions, each reconciled to its plan item. Month sets the plan against what happened, in aggregate. To walk through it on made-up data first, open the sample household on first run or under Setup.

Your ratio, on the future too

Needs, wants, savings: your 50/30/20 split, on the plan and the actuals. It measures balance, not amount, so it works at any income.

Savings

The future: retirement, brokerage, an emergency fund.

The Ratio card on Home and on Days shows Forecast, Current and the 50/30/20 Target side by side for the month you are looking at. The Forecast column comes from your plan, so a month’s split is there before its charges post.

Each figure says how it adds up: open a bucket to see its categories, largest first, each linked to its transactions or plan items. fi-fore states the measure, the standard and the gap. What to do about it is yours to decide.

Your machine, your database, free

The desktop app keeps your data in a database on your own computer, with no copy on fi-fore servers. No fi-fore account, no usage telemetry. Backup & Restore saves the whole database as one file you can take anywhere.

The desktop app has no subscription and no paid feature. What we won’t trade.

Who can read your money, and for how long

Where it runs Who can read it For how long
Desktop / self-host Can't read it. Only you, on hardware you own. No fi-fore account, no usage telemetry, no AI vendor in the path. As long as you keep the file. There is nothing to delete at fi-fore, because there is no copy.
Hosted (not available yet) Won't read it. We run the server, so we could; policy and controls stop us, not math. Until you delete the household, and no longer.

Desktop and self-host cost some convenience: you run it and back it up. Hosted trades some privacy for convenience: we run it.

Bank sync goes through SimpleFIN Bridge with your own token, and you pay SimpleFIN directly. SimpleFIN sees your transactions; fi-fore never sees your bank login. CSV import has no third party at all.

The desktop app checks fi-fore’s public release page for a newer version. That request carries no household data and no install ID, and you can turn it off in Setup.
